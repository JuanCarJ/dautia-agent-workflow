# Research and evidence

Use this when needs are uncertain or a study, benchmark, or simulation supports a
product claim. Do not add research before a defined authorized change unless an open
decision depends on it.

## Evidence discipline

- Start with the question; choose interview, observation, task test, card sorting, survey, analytics, benchmark, prototype, or simulation for what it can answer.
- Record actor/profile, task or episode, context, method, sample and selection, exclusions, denominator, assistance, simulated parts, and limits. Unknowns remain unknown.
- Separate what a participant said, what was observed, and what was inferred. Do not turn an observation into an invented motivation.
- Give neutral tasks that do not reveal controls. Record completion, error, recovery, abandonment, and moderator help; favorable opinion does not prove task success.
- Compare like states, data, tasks, and viewports, or state the confounder. Juxtaposed screenshots do not establish causality.
- Label expert review, QA, participant research, Wizard-of-Oz control, and agent simulation accurately. Simulated users expose coherence gaps but are not participants and yield no user success rate.
- Preserve date, population, sample, selection, and limitations when citing results. Do not transfer conversion, satisfaction, speed, or ROI to the current product without evidence.

## Sources and limits

- *Diseñando apps para móviles* (2013–2014), physical pp. 61–81 and 167–175: episodes, journeys, prototypes, and moderated tasks. Its 5–8 participant claim is not general proof.
- Multiplica, *Claves de la experiencia móvil perfecta* (2015), physical pp. 5 and 35–39: last-purchase interviews and method selection; its narrow 25-person sample does not represent all users or non-buyers.
- UXalliance/Usaria, *Delivery Apps en tiempos de COVID-19* (2020), physical pp. 13–17, 167, 186, 193, and 198–212: 47 selected app-country cases and a 331-person survey described as non-representative. Preserve incomplete attempts and exclusions.
- Cathy Pearl, *Designing Voice User Interfaces* (2016), physical pp. 183–207: task design, order effects, and Wizard of Oz. Thinking aloud during speech can contaminate recognition.
- *Design Thinking: Lidera el presente, crea el futuro*, physical pp. 49–74 and 126–135: observation, hypothesis, prototyping, and value lenses. Canvases and stages are optional tools, not gates or proof of demand.

These are historical sources. Verify current external facts or standards when the
task depends on them.
