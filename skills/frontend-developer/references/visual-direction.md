# Visual direction for open creative decisions

Focused reference: 2026-09-10.

Read this only when a new surface, a newly authorized identity/redesign, or a broad
visual improvement still has material creative decisions open. Skip it for a
focused delta, an established system with enough direction, or an audit that does
not authorize design changes.

## Preserve the real brief

Keep the user's original request, examples, constraints, and intended ambition
beside any implementation brief produced by the agent. A shorter technical brief
may clarify execution, but it does not silently reduce the desired character,
craft, scope, or quality bar. Treat an agent-authored concept as a proposal, never
as a user requirement, and mark assumptions and unresolved choices explicitly.

Classify the work before choosing a visual method:

- focused delta: execute the named change and preserve the accepted system;
- new surface in an established system: extend its identity and components;
- broad improvement without redesign: raise composition and finish inside the
  current identity;
- new identity or authorized redesign: establish a coherent direction before
  multiplying screens;
- audit: compare evidence and intent without changing the product.

## Resolve the direction

When direction is actually open, record only what will guide implementation:

- target person and the task or moment being served;
- intended perception or emotional register;
- central brand or product idea;
- primary expressive resource, such as photography, illustration, typography,
  spatial composition, motion, material texture, data, or 3D;
- composition type and hierarchy across the representative viewport;
- motion role, activation, pacing, and reduced-motion behavior;
- real constraints from content, platform, accessibility, performance, time, and
  available assets.

Choose the stack and asset technique after this direction is clear. Do not default
to plain HTML, system fonts, local fonts, or a no-network approach merely because
they are convenient for the implementer. Respect the project's stack and sourcing
rules, and choose what best carries the accepted direction within real constraints.

Photography, generated imagery, SVG, video, 3D, and Three.js are available tools,
not required ingredients. Use one when it contributes to meaning, identity, or
interaction strongly enough to justify its production, loading, and maintenance
cost. Record source, license or generation provenance, and intended use for every
material asset.

For Three.js or comparable WebGL work, provide a legible fallback when WebGL fails,
honor reduced motion, keep mobile complexity and device-pixel ratio reasonable,
pause rendering when off screen, and validate performance in proportion to the
surface's risk. The core content and action must survive without the effect.

## Inspect before multiplying

Build and inspect one representative composition early, in the actual candidate
when practical, before repeating its pattern across pages or sections. Check the
main viewport, a relevant mobile viewport, real content, hierarchy, crop, type,
motion, and recovery states. Iterate when the evidence reveals a problem.

This inspection is an execution checkpoint, not routine approval from the user and
not a mandatory number of variants. Ask only when a material unresolved choice
would change the result; otherwise use reversible design judgment and continue.

## Acceptance and record

Keep three questions separate:

- functional: can the intended actor complete the required task and recover?
- professional finish: is the composition coherent, responsive, accessible, and
  free of obvious unfinished states?
- ambition: does the result retain the character and quality promised by the
  original request rather than only satisfying an author-reduced brief?

Claims about user impact need observed behavior, research, or measured product
evidence. Do not turn taste, a mockup, or invented scores into proof of impact.

In the project's existing canonical source, document the concept and reasons,
asset types and provenance, composition choice, motion activation and reduced
behavior, preserved elements, and status as proposed, accepted, implemented,
verified, or deployed. Keep these states distinct and proportional to the change.
